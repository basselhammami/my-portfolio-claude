Where it all started

Balady+ is Saudi Arabia's official government maps and municipal services app — used by millions of citizens to navigate cities, report issues, and access government services.

My role focused on the core navigation experience: specifically the navigation mode and the introduction of a free roam feature that let users explore without needing a destination.

Three interconnected problems had gone unresolved, each making the map harder to use than it needed to be.

What I was solving for

The goal was to make the map feel less like a transactional tool and more like a spatial interface — fluid whether you were navigating or just exploring.

Why do I have to enter a destination just to look around?
Why does this feel harder than it needs to?

The core design challenges

  • Reduce cognitive load during active turn-by-turn navigation
  • Introduce exploration without breaking the navigation flow
  • Surface services faster — without deep menu hierarchies

"These weren't isolated UI problems — they reflected a fundamental mismatch between how users wanted to interact with the map and how the app was designed."

— Design insight, Balady+ discovery phase

How I approached it

Before designing anything, I mapped the complete user journey across every navigation scenario to understand exactly where and why the experience broke down. A service blueprint became the foundation for all three features.

The journey

  1. Discovery & field research

    Observed real navigation sessions and interviewed users to understand where the experience felt unnatural.

  2. Service blueprint

  3. Solution design

Service Blueprint

I mapped the complete user journey across every navigation scenario to identify where and why the experience broke down. This gave the team a shared understanding of the problem space before any solutions were explored.

Navigation Mode Redesign

The existing turn-by-turn navigation had too many elements competing for attention during active driving. The redesigned interface surfaces only what's needed at each moment — reducing cognitive load and mid-journey errors.

Parking Enhancements

Finding and navigating to parking was one of the most common urban use cases. The redesigned parking feature surfaces real-time availability, pricing, and distance — all without leaving the map. One tap to navigate.

Free Roam Mode

A new exploration mode that lets users browse the map without a fixed destination. The transition between free roam and navigation is seamless — tap any point of interest to get directions, or tap navigate to start from your current location.

Horizontal Navigation

A horizontal scrolling pattern for browsing service categories reduced the average number of taps from 4 to 2. Less hierarchy, faster discovery — without losing the depth the app needed.

What happened next

All three features shipped to millions of citizens across Saudi Arabia.

  • Significant reduction in navigation errors and mid-journey cancellations
  • Free roam introduced a new usage pattern — users exploring without a destination
  • Service discovery taps cut from 4 to 2
  • Parking flow fully integrated — no extra screen, no leaving the map

Looking back

What started as a navigation friction problem turned into a rethink of what a government map app could be.

The biggest design shift was recognising that the map was two products in one — a navigation tool and an exploration surface. Designing for both without creating tension between them was the real challenge.

It reinforced that scale doesn't make design harder — it makes clarity more important.
